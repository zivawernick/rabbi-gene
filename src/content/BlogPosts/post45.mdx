Ultra-Orthodox Jews will fight to the last breath not to fight to defend themselves or other inhabitants of Israel from foreign enemies that wish to wipe out all the inhabitants of Israel and Jews everywhere.

What the last election in Israel proved is that the Arab population is more than willing to be part of the community that supports the state.

It is time to set the record straight. Just because Ultra-Orthodox Jews dress up as for Halloween does not make them the true decision makers of how Jews should act in the present.

Jewish customs and ceremonies were always in flux changing with the times. The reform movement of the 19th century Europe drove a reaction on the part of mainly eastern-European rabbis to declare in the words of the Hatam Sofer of Hungary: "Everything new is forbidden."

The unfortunate response of the left-wing socialists in Europe was to admit that these "Ultra-Orthodox" represented the true Judaism as they tried to convert Jewish life and thought to the socialist cause. The result was that even non-Ultra-Orthodox Jews held those that espoused that way of life as being exemplars of what being really religious as Jews means.

In no way would Maimonides, Abraham ibn Ezra or even Rabbi Akiva would acknowledge the lifestyle of "Ultra-Orthodox" Jews as being the way and the only way for Jews in thought and action.

None of the great sages of Israel though they might live in a physical ghetto never lived in a ghetto of the mind! To avoid meaningful education that includes the latest in science, to avoid the rabbinic dictum that the best way is: "Torah with the natural way of earning a living and being in the world" is not what the great sages of Israel have preached and practiced. Rabban Gamliel had a dual curriculum in his academy half of the students learning Greek Philosophy! And our main prayers are the creation of his academy!

Why Jews and Judaism have survived is because we were able in each age to reinvent themselves to retain the old and incorporate the best of the zeitgeist.

It is time to remove the luster from anachronism and face the future with respect for the past and adaptation as it is needed for the continuity of the tradition from Sinai that renews and refreshes itself in every generation.

Shabbat Shalom

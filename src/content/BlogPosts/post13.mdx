Shabbat Shalom!

This week’s Torah portion is unique in that it establishes the ancestors of those now known as Jews as invaders of the lan of Israel.  Hesitant ones at that.  Moses according to the story sends spies to test the land tht has been promised to them by their God.  Ten of the spies come back saying it really is a wonderful place, but we don’t stand a chance of conquering it.  The inhabitants are too strong for us.

So the people spend forty years in Sinai until a new generation has the courage to conquer the land. The biblical account has been questioned in the modern period.

One Israeli scholar has even questioned if the Israeli population ever was in Egypt but that there was a slave revolt that overturned the previous order.

What does remain after all the questions as to Biblical authenticity is that by the twelfth century BCE at least the present ethnic group now known as Jews were in possession of the land.  It was the same group tht were carried off in part by Assyrian and Babylonian exiles and returned in 538 BCE.

How do we know for sure that those who would then be called Jews returned?

There is a cuneiform cylinder in the British Museum (I saw it in the Metropolitan when it came on loan) that is the edict of Cyrus conqueror of Babylonia giving permission for the exiles to return to their native land.  Cyrus gave them money and furniture of their destroyed Temple to rebuild it.

Why were these people called now Jews?  Because the province of the Persian Empire they inhabited was called-YAHUD.  Every male living in this province from then on was known as a Yehudi-German Jude (pronounced Yude), English Jew.

So, no matter who thereafter conquered or invaded the land Jews existed from that time as the inhabitants thereof.  The return was by law of the Persians, those who returned were not colonists, but legal inhabitants thereof recognized by the Persian Empire as the rightful citizens of that real estate!

The land was conquered by the Romans, ruled by the Byzantines, conquered by the Arabs, invaded by the Crusaders, ruled by the Ottomans, conquered by the British and in 1947 recognized as the Jewish State of Israel by the United Nations.

But throughout its history the Jews were the only original inhabitants who consistently throughout the long centuries lived there in continuous settlement.

At times flourishing, at times diminished and persecuted but always there in numbers sometimes small and then increased.

The Arabs were the colonists, and they came in increased numbers when Jews returned to turn the backwater into a flourishing society.   The British reports of the 1920’s and 30’s when Jewish immigration was restricted noted that there was unrestricted Arab immigration to then known Palestine.

The city of Jerusalem always had a plurality of Jewish inhabitants as well as Muslim and Christian.

The well-funded propaganda on the part of antisemites of various denominations and political persuasions, including academics with prejudice aforethought to cast Jews as colonists of their native land is the lie of this century.

Shabbat Shalom

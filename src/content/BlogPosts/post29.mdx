Dear Friends,

One greets one another at the end of the week with "Shabbat Shalom." Traditionally in a society in which hard labor was the rule the approach of respite from toil was greeted with joy and celebration.

Today for most hard labor is not the rule but a chance for relaxation and "fun" is most welcome.

Yet the news bursts into our lives with glaring headlines that remind us that whatever joy we can anticipate is clouded by news that either saddens or infuriates us.

The latest headline added to the too many that troubled me was the intention of some senators to indite Anthony Fauci for contempt of Congress.

The "Fauci diaries" that he was careless to leave on a government server are filled with the wonder and pride that engulfed him as he was looked upon as a hero during the Covid Crisis. A human failing that so many cannot resist.

What saddens me most and I do not attempt to cast either party as to fault or to blame, is that these congressional hearings have become showcases for grandstanding upon the part of those who are elected to serve us, the citizens of these United States.

The Covid Epidemic was an unprecedented challenge in our day. Fortunately, it was not as deadly as it could have been. The vaccination that was developed with unbelievable speed is to be heralded as a tremendous achievement of all, scientists, government officials, and yes Anthony Fauci and Donald Trump.

Mistakes were made, false starts were initiated, but the world was saved from what could have rivaled the Black Plague of the Middle Ages in death and societal disruption.

What is needed is not looking for blame but for rational steps that can make us better prepared for future threats. And unfortunately, future threats are not fear mongering but the reality of living in a world that becomes more complicated and more likely for the swift transmission of a medical emergency from remote recesses to population centers.

Our world has grown smaller as to what happens in the Amazon can almost immediately have an effect in London, New York and Paris.

Better than trying to find fault is to search for answers to problems that surely will arise.

As John Donne wrote: *"And therefore never send to know for whom the bell tolls; it tolls for thee."*

Shabbat Shalom

Love

Gene

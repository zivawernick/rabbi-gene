Dear Friends,

One of the deficits of the present moment is that we are lacking in the world of our intellectual and spiritual fields a voice with sufficient heft to command the attention of all of us. We are ourselves divided into so many hyphenated groups that at times we lose the central message of Jewish history that at the time we need to be united the most we are divided the most.

There are members of the tribe who are driven by the need to be affiliated with those whose central message is at odds with our need for self-preservation in a western world in which antisemitism is transmitted through the mother's milk of the dominant religions and culture in which we dwell.

Jews are strangers that may be accepted but never really integrated into western society. We may dress, affiliate and integrate as best we can but if we do not convert and grow an intellectual foreskin, we will never be fully accepted but at best tolerated. Every hatred is abhorred by the liberal intellectual class except antisemitism which is excused and is demanded to be excused by those Jews who in the depths of their self-abnegation cast their lot with those who are the admitted enemies of the state that was created as a safety net for us when all else failed.

The reality is that Jews are as our Bible states, "a people who dwell apart." We do not accept the pieties of those among whom we dwell. Not that we are asocial as claimed. It is because we hold our truths to be real and the claims of others to veracity to be false. Our ancient teachings preceded all that the west now holds dear. We believe in a future that holds a promise for peace. But that promise must be based on truth, justice and respect for all that lives.

That is our lot. To deny it is to orphan oneself from your mother's breast and drink a sour poison that slowly will rot your self-esteem.

It is a bitter choice for those who are not learned in our tradition and waver in their allegiance to the sacred doctrines and mores that have carried us from Sinai to today. But for us that hold to the teachings of our tradition and proudly lay claim to be descendants by birth or by choice to the response of our ancestors at Sinai; who upon hearing those Ten Sentences said: "We will do what we hear," we bear with pride the slings and arrows of outrageous fortune and pray for that day when our dream of peace for all on earth may be accomplished.

Shabbat Shalom

Gene

Dear Friends,

First Shabbat Shalom.

With the fast of the 17th of Tammuz we begin the “Three Weeks” in which Jews observe a period of mourning for the destruction of the Temple in Jerusalem.

The loss of the Temples was more than the loss of the focal point of Jewish worship, they also entailed the loss of national sovereignty and years of exile that besides the lack of political self-determination were accompanied by the fiercest persecution any group has experienced during their long history.

Recently genetic studies have proven the unbroken link of Jews no matter where they have lived or no matter what sub-group they belong to have an unbroken genetic link to the land of Israel.  With the reestablishment of a Jewish presence in its native land the question of the observance of national mourning for a destruction that was hundreds of years in the past and has been superseded in horror by the Holocaust should be worthy of national commemoration.

I for one believe that these past destructions are more important today than in years past.  Not that the Temple should be rebuilt.  Fortunately, we have advanced in our spiritual sensitivities to realize that prayer and study are more worthy pursuits of spiritual and moral excellence than the slaughter of animals with the concomitant streams of blood and heaps of manure!

But the analysis of what were the underlying factors that brought about the destruction of both Temples has an important lesson for today.

In both cases, arrogant supposition that a small but powerful nation could withstand the might of world empires brought about not only the destruction of the independent polity, the slaughter of so many and the many years of exile that this nonsensical idea of religious zealotry in the face of overwhelming foreign might could prevail.

Today, the despicable and vile acts of Israeli fanatics in the West Bank and the hallucinations of some like Ben Gevir of rebuilding the Temple on the site of two mosques endanger the future security and well-being of a nation reborn that has up to recently been a leader in scientific and medical research and a loyal partner to the Free World.

“Those who do not learn from the past are destined to repeat it.”  That is not a quote without meaning and significance especially in these times.

With the outrageous statements emanating from the present government, with the unrestricted atrocities of fanatics who are tacitly if not outright supported, with the grift and graft of a religious establishment and an unwillingness to come to terms with the full incorporation of all citizens into the body politic despite whatever their religious or ethnic background, I fear for the future of the land to which I have given my life’s work to support.

I have led two marches, to Washington, and raised millions of dollars to support the revival of a Jewish State, in the Jewish Land with a call once again to be a “Light unto the Nations.”

I therefor think that the fasts, the lamentations and the prayers of these three weeks are a cause for Repentance and self-evaluation that should be undertaken by all who rejoice in the Israel of today and pray for the Israel of tomorrow.

The Baal Shem Tov is quoted as asking the Messiah, “when will the Master come?’  The reply was: “Tomorrow if you will it!”

Again Shabbat Shalom

Love

Gene

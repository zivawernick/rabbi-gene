Dear Friends,

On this coming Sunday we will finish the official reading in public of the Torah (first five Books of the Bible) and begin again the reading thereof. It is symbolic that every time we read or listen to the reading we are engaged in reinterpreting a message from long ago to be applicable to our time.

The rabbis themselves were fond of saying "Dor Dor V'Dorshav" (Each generation and its interpreters). The search for moral truth is not fixed in time. Each generation is commanded to sift through the teachings of the past and apply them best to the situations of the present.

Hopefully our moral horizons have broadened and will continue to expand. What was accepted in the past does not bind us to views constricted by time and place.

Plato opined whether it was better to have wise and moral men or moral laws. We have been taught by experience that both are necessary. Moral Philosophy as enunciated by Immanuel Kant demands "Vernunft" a deep insight as to what will be the ultimate benefit.

As we approach the challenges of "AI" it is imperative for there to be an international community of scholars who will determine its uses so as not to have it present a danger to all humanity and the world on which we dwell.

The midrash in Vayikra Rabbah that comes to mind is the tale told of people in a boat. One of them is digging a hole in the bottom. What are you doing, the others cry. I am digging in my own spot, he replies. Yes, but you will sink us all, they respond!

Let us hope and pray that moral people with moral goals will control the possibilities that challenge our future on this earth, for us and all that the world contains.

Shabbat Shalom

Love Gene

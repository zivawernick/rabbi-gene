Rabbi Gene turns to the fourth order of the Mishnah, Nezikin — damages — which he calls one of the most consequential of the six orders because it governs how Jews are meant to deal with one another in ordinary civil life: torts, oaths, courts, and idolatry. He traces the practice of swearing on a Bible in Western courts back to a thirteenth-century Castilian law code, and shows how a stone-throwing custom dedicated to the Greek god Hermes quietly survived into the Jewish practice of leaving stones on a grave.

The teaching closes with tractate Avot and its best-known line, that the world stands on three things — Torah, service, and acts of loving-kindness — along with a warning, drawn from Nezikin's tractates on courts, about what happens to any society that lets its justice system be corrupted.

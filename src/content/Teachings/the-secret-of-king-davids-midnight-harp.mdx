For Shabbat Pinchas, Rabbi Gene turns to the Aggadata, the story-telling
layer woven through the Talmud, as it appears in the Yerushalmi, the
Jerusalem Talmud compiled largely in Tiberias and Caesarea. Starting from a
halakhic discussion of when the Shema and Amidah should be recited, he
follows the rabbis as they build a legend around a single verse from Psalm
57: that King David hung a harp over his bed, and each midnight the wind
rustled its strings and woke him to sing praises to God while other kings
slept in. The teaching closes with the congregation learning the joyful
melody "Urah Kevodi," set to that very verse.

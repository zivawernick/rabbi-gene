In this Passover teaching, Rabbi Gene traces the surprising migration of the
Kalonymus family — a name that means "good name" in Greek — from the Byzantine
Empire through Lucca and up into the German Rhineland. Along the way, he
explains how Charlemagne's invitation to Jewish merchants around 800 CE
seeded the communities of Speyer, Worms, and Mainz, together known as ShUM,
laying the foundations of Ashkenazic Jewry. It's a lesson in how migration
and geography, as much as theology, shaped the divide between Ashkenazi and
Sephardic custom.

As the season of Selichot arrives, Rabbi Gene introduces the congregation to the specific penitential hymn, or pizmon, chanted on the eve of Rosh Hashanah, tracing its language and its refrain. From there he turns to its author, Rabbeinu Gershom ben Yehuda, known to history as Meor HaGolah, "the Light of the Exile" — the tenth-century sage of Mainz whose rulings on monogamy, divorce, and the privacy of correspondence quietly built the legal foundation of Ashkenazic Jewry. He closes with a personal and little-known detail of Rabbeinu Gershom's own life: a son who converted to Christianity, and the grief that followed.

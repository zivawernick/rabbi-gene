Working through Seder Taharot, the Mishnah's order of ritual purity, Rabbi
Gene turns first to Taharat HaMishpacha, the laws of family purity that
still shape observant Jewish marriages, before turning to that week's Torah
portion and its strange, ash-and-water ritual of the red heifer. He then
brings in a Midrash from Bamidbar Rabbah in which a skeptical Greek
philosopher challenges Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai to defend a ritual that
looks like magic — setting up a broader reflection on how the sages who
rebuilt Jewish life after the Temple's destruction turned inherited custom
into a rational, ethical way of life.

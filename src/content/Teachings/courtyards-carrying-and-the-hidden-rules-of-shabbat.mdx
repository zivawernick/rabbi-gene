Continuing his walk through the Mishnah with the congregation, Rabbi Gene moves into the order of Moed and its opening tractates, Shabbat and Eruvin, to explain a piece of halachah most people never think about: the rules governing where one may and may not carry on Shabbat. He traces how the rabbis' concept of the eruv — a symbolic device using a shared meal, a lintel and a pole, or a continuous string — lets communities redraw the boundaries between private and public space so that ordinary life can continue. Along the way he points out that the same mechanism still encircles Manhattan and the Old City of Jerusalem today.

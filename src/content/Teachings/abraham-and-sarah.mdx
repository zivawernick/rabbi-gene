Working from a little-known medieval midrash on the moment Sarah learns she will bear a child, Rabbi Gene builds outward into a wide-ranging meditation on marriage, moral courage, and partnership. He makes the case that Abraham and Sarah are, in his reading, the one genuinely happy marriage in the entire Bible, and leans on Rashi's striking claim that Sarah was in fact the greater prophet of the two. Along the way he reflects on what modern couples have lost — a surrounding community that once taught people how to live with one another — and on what Abraham and Sarah's devotion still has to teach.

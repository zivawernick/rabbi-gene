Having worked through the great Midrashim and arrived at tractate Berachot, Rabbi Gene turns to the centerpiece of daily Jewish prayer: the Amidah, also known as the Shemoneh Esrei, the eighteen blessings. He traces its authorship to Shimon HaPakoli, who ordered the blessings before Rabban Gamliel at Yavneh, and makes the case that Yavneh — not Jerusalem — is where the Judaism practiced today actually took shape, forged by rabbis rebuilding prayer from nothing after the Temple's destruction.

The teaching moves blessing by blessing through Avot, Gevurot, and Kedushah, pairing each with its biblical roots — the patriarchs and matriarchs, the doctrine of resurrection, and the visions of Isaiah and Ezekiel that gave the world the words "holy, holy, holy." It closes on a personal note, on what it means to imagine the divine and why a people so often threatened has kept returning to that vision generation after generation.

Continuing through Seder Nashim, the order of the Mishnah devoted to women,
Rabbi Gene walks the congregation line by line through the Ketubah, the
traditional Jewish marriage contract, and the earlier, distinct stage of
Kiddushin that precedes it. He details the husband's binding obligations —
food, clothing, and coital rights recognized in Jewish law long before
comparable protections existed in American courts — and shares cases,
including one of his own from Canada, where rabbinic courts intervened to
protect a wife from being held hostage over a Jewish divorce. It's a teaching
about how, in his reading, Jewish legal tradition has consistently sided
with the vulnerable over the powerful.
